Hands-On Lab 1

The Incident War Room

Turn raw incident chaos into a structured post-incident review, root cause analysis, and executive summary — in 15 minutes instead of 3 days.

Scenario

Production is Down

It's 2:02 PM UTC on a Thursday. PagerDuty fires — your orders API is returning 5xx errors, latency is through the roof, and customer tickets are flooding in. You're the on-call engineer. You have raw logs, alerts, and a panicked Slack channel. You need to make sense of the chaos, fix it, and then explain what happened — all before the executive team's 4 PM standup.

The Old Way

Manually sift through logs, build a timeline in a Google Doc, write the PIR over the next 2 days, schedule a meeting to review. Elapsed: 2–3 days.

The AI Way

Upload the incident log and service map to Claude. Get a structured timeline, root cause analysis, PIR, and executive summary in 15 minutes.

Problem

What You Need to Deliver

Using the sample input files provided below, you will use Claude to create the following four deliverables:

  1. A structured incident timeline — what happened, when, and in what order
  2. A root cause analysis — the technical "why" with evidence from the logs
  3. A blameless Post-Incident Review (PIR) — with contributing factors, action items, and systemic improvements
  4. An executive summary — non-technical, focused on business impact and prevention
Data

Incident Data Files

Two files are provided for this lab. Download them to your computer, then upload them to Claude in the next section.

File Description
sample-incident-log.txt Contains alerts, engineer chat log, deployment history, and customer impact data
sample-service-map.txt Architecture context: service dependencies, infrastructure details, and SLOs

Steps

How to Run This Lab

Follow these steps in order. Each step builds on the previous one, using the same Claude conversation thread throughout.

1

Open claude.ai

Go to claude.ai and start a new conversation. Make sure you're using Claude (Sonnet or Opus).

2

Upload the Data

Upload both files — sample-incident-log.txt and sample-service-map.txt — into Claude. You can drag and drop them or use the attachment button. Alternatively, paste their contents directly into the chat.

3

Prompt #1 — Build the Incident Timeline

With the files uploaded, send the following prompt to Claude:

Prompt #1 — Incident Timeline 
I've uploaded an incident log and a service dependency map for a production outage (INC-2026-0417).

Build a structured incident timeline from the data. For each entry include:
1. Timestamp (UTC)
2. What happened (one sentence)
3. Category: [Alert | Human Action | System Event | Decision]
4. Severity impact at that moment (Escalating / Stable / Recovering)

Sort chronologically. Highlight the TRIGGER event and the RESOLUTION event.
At the bottom, give me: Time to Detect, Time to Mitigate, and Total Customer Impact Duration.

Format as a clean table.
4

Prompt #2 — Root Cause Analysis + PIR

In the same conversation, send this follow-up prompt:

Prompt #2 — Blameless PIR 
Now write a blameless Post-Incident Review (PIR) for this incident. Use the following structure:

1. INCIDENT SUMMARY (2-3 sentences: what happened, impact, duration)
2. TIMELINE (reference the timeline you just built)
3. ROOT CAUSE (technical explanation — what specifically caused the failure, with evidence from the logs)
4. CONTRIBUTING FACTORS (systemic issues that allowed this to happen — NOT who did what wrong):
   - Process factors (deployment practices, review gaps)
   - Technical factors (architecture, monitoring, safeguards)
   - Organizational factors (on-call, communication)
5. WHAT WENT WELL (things that worked during the response)
6. ACTION ITEMS — table format: Action | Owner (leave as [TBD]) | Priority (P0/P1/P2) | Due Date (relative)
   Include at least 6 action items covering: deployment process, monitoring, architecture, and testing.
7. LESSONS LEARNED (3 key takeaways for the team)

Tone: factual, blameless, focused on systems not people. This will be shared with the entire engineering org.
5

Prompt #3 — Executive Summary

Still in the same conversation, send the final prompt:

Prompt #3 — Executive Summary 
Now write a non-technical executive summary of this incident for our VP of Engineering and CTO. They have 2 minutes to read this.

Include:
1. HEADLINE: One sentence — what happened and is it fixed?
2. BUSINESS IMPACT: Customer-facing duration, estimated revenue impact, customer tickets
3. ROOT CAUSE: Explain in plain English — no jargon, no service names. A business leader should understand it.
4. WHAT WE'RE DOING ABOUT IT: Top 3 actions, written as commitments ("We will..." not "We should...")
5. RISK OF RECURRENCE: Low / Medium / High — with a one-line justification

Keep it under 250 words. Tone: confident, transparent, accountable.
6

Review & Iterate

Review all three outputs. Check the timeline for accuracy, verify the root cause makes sense, and confirm the executive summary avoids jargon. Ask Claude to adjust anything that needs refinement. Compare your results with your lab partner — did Claude produce similar analyses? Where did they differ?

Solution Walkthrough — Admin Only

Complete the lab first! The facilitator will review the solution with you.

Solution

What Good Looks Like

Use this evaluation guidance to assess the quality of each deliverable. The specifics below reflect what a strong response should include based on the sample data.

Solution Walkthrough

Timeline Evaluation

The incident timeline should clearly show the following key events:

  • Trigger at 13:48 UTC — Deployment of order-service v2.14.0 pushed via ArgoCD
  • Detection at 14:02 UTC — First PagerDuty alert fires (5xx errors on orders API)
  • Escalation at 14:06 UTC — On-call engineer escalates to team lead, Slack war room opened
  • Mitigation at 14:10 UTC — Rollback initiated to order-service v2.13.2
  • Resolution at 14:20 UTC — Rollback complete, error rates return to baseline

Key metrics: Time to Detect (TTD): ~14 minutes, Time to Mitigate (TTM): ~18 minutes from trigger. Total customer impact duration should be calculated from first customer-visible errors to resolution.

Root Cause Evaluation

The root cause analysis should identify that order-service v2.14.0 changed from cached inventory reads (5-minute refresh cycle) to real-time database queries on every order read. This tripled database connections from approximately 60 to 200, causing PostgreSQL connection pool exhaustion. The analysis should also note that the deployment was pushed as a full rollout with no canary phase, so the problem hit 100% of traffic immediately.

PIR Action Items

Strong action items should include at minimum:

  • Enforce canary deployments for all production services
  • Add database connection pool alerting at 80% threshold
  • Implement load testing as a CI/CD pipeline gate
  • Add circuit breaker pattern on order-service database calls
  • Increase database connection pool limits or add PgBouncer as a connection pooler
  • Establish deployment freeze windows during peak business hours

Executive Summary Evaluation

The executive summary should NOT mention service names like "order-service," "ArgoCD," "PostgreSQL," or "PgBouncer." Instead, it should translate to business language:

  • "order-service" becomes "our ordering system"
  • "ArgoCD deployment" becomes "a software update"
  • "DB connection pool exhaustion" becomes "the system was overwhelmed by the number of requests it was trying to process simultaneously"

Evaluation Checklist

  • Does the timeline accurately reflect the log data?
  • Is the root cause technically correct?
  • Is the PIR genuinely blameless (no "Engineer X should have..." language)?
  • Are action items specific and measurable?
  • Could the executive summary be understood by a non-technical board member?

Wow Realization

You just built a complete post-incident package — timeline, PIR, and executive brief — in under 15 minutes. This normally takes 2–3 days of async work across multiple engineers. The quality is consistent, the format is standardized, and the blameless tone is maintained throughout.

Takeaway

Key Takeaway

Before AI

  • PIRs take 2–3 days to write
  • Format is inconsistent across teams and incidents
  • Often delayed, sometimes indefinitely
  • Executive summaries get skipped entirely
  • Action items are vague ("improve monitoring")

After AI

  • Upload the incident data, get a structured PIR in minutes
  • Format is standardized and complete every time
  • Available the same day as the incident
  • Executive summary is board-ready by default
  • Action items are specific ("Add DB connection pool alerting at 80% threshold")

AI doesn't replace the humans in the room — it eliminates the 3-day gap between incident and insight. The conversation about what to fix can happen the same day.
