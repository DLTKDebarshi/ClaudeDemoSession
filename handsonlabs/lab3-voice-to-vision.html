Hands-On Lab 3

Voice to Vision

Turn messy whiteboard scribbles or a rambling voice memo into a professional Org Chart and team announcement — in your leadership tone.

Scenario

The Post-Meeting Mess

You just walked out of a high-stakes 1:1 with your CEO. The two of you mapped out a new department structure on a whiteboard — boxes, arrows, scribbled names, crossed-out ideas. It made perfect sense in the room.

Now you need to:

  1. Turn those scribbles into a professional Org Chart you can present to the leadership team
  2. Draft an announcement email to the broader team explaining the new structure
  3. Do both in your specific leadership tone — not generic corporate-speak
This lab proves that Claude understands intent, not just text.

It's a "Thought Translator" — taking unstructured, messy human thinking and turning it into polished, professional output.

The Problem

What You Need to Deliver

  1. Upload a messy input — a photo of whiteboard scribbles OR a rough voice memo transcript
  2. Generate a clean Org Chart as a Claude Artifact (visual, not just text)
  3. Draft an announcement email that explains the changes in a specific leadership tone
  4. Iterate until it matches what was actually discussed (not what Claude assumes)

Step-by-Step

How to Run This Lab

1

Open claude.ai

Start a new conversation. If using Option A, prepare to upload the whiteboard screenshot.

2

Upload or Paste Your Input

Option A: Screenshot the whiteboard mock above and drag it into Claude.
Option B: Copy-paste the voice memo transcript into Claude.

3

Prompt #1 — The Org Chart

Ask Claude to interpret the messy input and create a professional visual.

Prompt #1 — Org Chart (Option A: Photo)I just took this photo of a whiteboard from a planning meeting with my CEO. It maps out a new department structure for Cloud Operations. Please: 1. Interpret the whiteboard — figure out the reporting structure, new roles, and key decisions. 2. Create a professional Org Chart as an Artifact — clean boxes, clear reporting lines, and color-coded to distinguish existing roles (blue), new roles to be hired (orange), and roles pending HR approval (gray). 3. Add notes below the chart for: dotted-line relationships, teams being moved, and any open decisions from the whiteboard. Make it presentation-ready — I'm sharing this with the leadership team on Friday.
Prompt #1 — Org Chart (Option B: Voice Memo)Here's a voice memo transcript I recorded right after a planning meeting with my CEO about a Cloud Operations reorg. It's rambling and unstructured — that's fine, I need you to make sense of it. Please: 1. Extract the org structure from my stream-of-consciousness notes. 2. Create a professional Org Chart as an Artifact — clean boxes, clear reporting lines, and color-coded: existing roles (blue), new roles to be hired (orange), and roles pending HR approval (gray). 3. Add notes below the chart for: dotted-line relationships, teams being moved, and anything I flagged as sensitive or pending. 4. List any ambiguities — things that weren't clear from my memo that I should clarify before presenting. Make it presentation-ready for the leadership team.
4

Prompt #2 — The Announcement Email

Once the org chart looks right, ask for the communication piece.

Prompt #2 — Announcement EmailNow draft the team announcement email for this reorg. Here are my constraints: Tone: Warm but direct. My CEO's exact words: "Write it like you're telling a friend, not like you're writing a press release." No corporate jargon. No buzzwords. What to include: - Why we're making this change (growth, operational clarity) - The new structure (high-level, link to the full org chart) - What's changing for existing team members (be honest) - What's NOT changing (reassure people) - Timeline (announce by end of April, effective Q3) What to EXCLUDE: - Do NOT name Priya or her promotion — HR hasn't approved the title change yet - Do NOT mention the VP role is being hired externally — say "we're launching a search" - Keep it under 400 words Sign it from me — use the name "[Your Name]" as a placeholder.
5

Prompt #3 — The Sensitivity Check

Leaders need to be careful about what gets communicated. Ask Claude to audit its own output.

Prompt #3 — Sensitivity AuditBefore I send this, audit the email and org chart for anything I asked you to exclude: 1. Does the email or chart mention Priya's name or her specific promotion? 2. Does it reveal that the VP role will be hired externally? 3. Is there anything that could be misread as a layoff or role elimination? 4. Does the tone match "telling a friend" or did it drift into corporate-speak? Flag anything that needs fixing and suggest specific rewrites.
6

Review, Iterate, Share

Polish the outputs. Try: "Make the org chart wider," "Add a FAQ section to the email," "Translate the email to Spanish for the LATAM team." Share with your lab partner.

Solution Walkthrough

What Good Looks Like

Reveal the Solution & Evaluation Guide

Expected Org Chart

Claude should generate an Artifact with a visual org chart. Here's the structure to verify:

Correct Structure: 
VP Cloud Operations [ORANGE - New, to be hired]
  |
  |--- Platform Engineering [BLUE - Existing]
  |      Lead: Marcus T.
  |      Contains: DevOps (separate team, NOT merged)
  |
  |--- Site Reliability Engineering [GRAY - Pending HR]
  |      Lead: [Pending HR Approval]
  |      Contains: Observability (moved FROM Platform)
  |
  |--- Cloud Security [ORANGE - New team]
         3 hires needed
         Dotted line to CISO

Common mistakes to watch for:

  • Merging DevOps into Platform — the whiteboard explicitly decided against this
  • Keeping Observability under Platform — it should move to SRE
  • Naming Priya as SRE lead — should say "Pending HR Approval" or similar
  • Missing the dotted-line from Cloud Security to CISO
  • Showing the VP role as filled — should be marked as open/hiring

Expected Announcement Email

A strong email will:

  • Lead with the "why": "As our cloud infrastructure has grown, so has the need for clearer ownership and dedicated focus areas..."
  • Be conversational: Short sentences. First-person. No "synergies" or "leveraging" or "strategic alignment."
  • Name what's staying the same: "Platform Engineering stays exactly as it is. Marcus continues to lead. Your day-to-day doesn't change."
  • Be honest about the new VP role: "We're launching a search for a VP of Cloud Operations" — NOT "we're hiring externally."
  • Handle the SRE leadership carefully: "We'll share more about SRE leadership in the coming weeks" — no mention of Priya or promotion.
  • End with an open door: "If you have questions, grab time on my calendar. I mean it."
  • Stay under 400 words.

Expected Sensitivity Audit

Claude should catch and flag:

  • Any instance where Priya's name appears (it shouldn't)
  • Any language implying external hire for the VP role
  • Any phrasing that could be read as "your team is being dissolved" (especially around the Observability move)
  • Any drift into corporate jargon that contradicts the "tell a friend" instruction

Bonus: Ambiguities Claude Should Flag

If using the voice memo, Claude should identify these open questions:

  • "Should we still post the VP role internally first?" — you mentioned it but didn't confirm
  • "What's the reporting structure for DevOps within Platform?" — you kept it separate but didn't name a lead
  • "Does the Observability team move to SRE immediately or at Q3 start?" — timeline is ambiguous
  • "Who approves the 3 Cloud Security hires — the new VP or existing leadership?" — chicken-and-egg problem

Evaluation Checklist

  • Did the Org Chart render as a visual Artifact (not just a text list)?
  • Is the color-coding correct (blue/orange/gray)?
  • Did Claude correctly interpret the crossed-out "merge DevOps" decision?
  • Is the Observability team under SRE, not Platform?
  • Does the email stay under 400 words?
  • Is Priya's name absent from all outputs?
  • Does the tone sound like a real person, not a press release?
  • Did the sensitivity audit catch real issues (not just generic warnings)?
The "Wow" Realization

You just turned 2 minutes of unstructured, messy, human thinking — scribbles on a whiteboard or a rambling voice note — into a presentation-ready org chart, a carefully worded announcement, and a sensitivity audit that caught political landmines.

Claude didn't just process text. It understood intent, respected constraints, and translated your thinking into professional output — while knowing what NOT to say.

This is what transforms AI from a "writing tool" into a "Thought Translator."

Key Takeaway

Why This Is a Game Changer

Before

You take a photo of the whiteboard. It sits in your camera roll for two days. You finally open PowerPoint, recreate the boxes, draft the email, get nervous about what to include, run it by a colleague, revise it, and send it 5 days later. The momentum from the meeting is gone.

After

You upload the photo (or paste your voice notes) while walking back from the meeting. By the time you sit down at your desk, you have a polished org chart, a draft email, and a sensitivity audit. You send it the same afternoon. The momentum holds.

For leaders who think in conversations, not documents — Claude bridges the gap between what's in your head and what needs to be on the page.
