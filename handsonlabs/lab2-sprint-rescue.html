Hands-On Lab 2

The Sprint Rescue

Use Claude to diagnose a failing sprint, build a data-driven re-plan, and draft a stakeholder update — in 15 minutes instead of half a day.

Scenario

Sprint 14 Is Going Sideways

It's Wednesday of Sprint 14 — 3 days before sprint end. Your team of 5 engineers committed to 52 story points (37 new + 15 carry-over from the disastrous Sprint 13). Only 10 points are Done. 3 stories are blocked. 4 tickets are unassigned. The Product Owner is asking "Are we going to hit our sprint goal?" and the VP of Engineering wants a status update by end of day.

You need to assess the situation, make hard calls about what to cut, and communicate clearly — fast.

The Old Way Spend 2 hours in ad-hoc Slack conversations, manually calculate velocity, write a status email that takes 45 minutes to draft, and still not have a clear plan. Elapsed: half a day.
The AI Way Upload your backlog export and sprint history to Claude. Get a sprint health diagnosis, a re-plan, and a stakeholder update in 15 minutes.
Problem Statement

What You Need to Deliver

  1. A sprint health diagnosis — is this sprint salvageable? What does the data say?
  2. A re-plan recommendation — which stories to keep, cut, or descope, with rationale
  3. A stakeholder update email — honest, clear, and constructive for the VP of Engineering
Target Roles Scrum Master • Project Manager • Team Lead
Time to Complete 15–20 minutes

Step-by-Step

How to Run This Lab

1

Open claude.ai

Go to claude.ai and start a new conversation. Any Claude model will work for this lab.

2

Upload the Data

Upload both CSV files (sample-backlog.csv and sample-sprint-history.csv) using the attachment button in Claude, or paste their contents directly into the chat.

3

Prompt #1 — Sprint Health Diagnosis

Paste the following prompt to get a data-driven assessment of Sprint 14's health.

Prompt #1 — Sprint Health DiagnosisI've uploaded our Sprint 14 backlog and sprint history data. We're a 5-person engineering team, 3 days from sprint end. Analyse the sprint health and give me: 1. VELOCITY ANALYSIS: What's our average velocity over the last 5 sprints? What's realistic to complete in the remaining 3 days? 2. SPRINT 14 STATUS: Break down the 52 planned points by status (Done, In Progress, To Do, Blocked, Carry-Over). What percentage is complete? 3. RISK ASSESSMENT: - Which stories are at risk and why? (blocked, unassigned, high points with low progress) - How does Sprint 14's plan compare to our actual velocity? Was this sprint over-committed from the start? 4. CARRY-OVER DEBT: We're carrying over from Sprint 13. Is this becoming a pattern? What does the trend show? 5. DIAGNOSIS: In 2-3 sentences, what's the honest assessment? Is this sprint salvageable? Be data-driven. Show your math. Don't sugarcoat it.
4

Prompt #2 — Re-Plan Recommendation

Once you have the diagnosis, ask Claude to build a concrete re-plan for the remaining 3 days.

Prompt #2 — Re-Plan RecommendationBased on your diagnosis, create a re-plan recommendation for the remaining 3 days of Sprint 14. For each of the 15 tickets, recommend one of: - KEEP: Realistic to complete in 3 days. State why. - CUT: Remove from sprint. State why and which sprint to move it to. - DESCOPE: Reduce scope to deliver partial value. State what to cut from the story. Format as a table: Ticket ID | Title | Points | Current Status | Recommendation | Rationale Then give me: 1. REVISED SPRINT GOAL: What can we realistically commit to delivering? 2. REVISED POINT TARGET: How many points is the new plan? 3. ASSIGNMENT GAPS: Are any kept stories missing an assignee? Suggest reassignments based on workload. 4. BLOCKED ITEM RESOLUTION: For PROJ-307 (blocked), suggest an unblock strategy or recommend cutting it. End with a "Sprint 14 Commitment Statement" — one paragraph the Scrum Master can read at standup tomorrow.
5

Prompt #3 — Stakeholder Update Email

Now draft a professional stakeholder update for the VP of Engineering.

Prompt #3 — Stakeholder Update EmailWrite a stakeholder update email to our VP of Engineering about Sprint 14's status. This person reads 20 emails a day — be concise and honest. Include: 1. SUBJECT LINE: Clear and informative (not "Sprint Update" — something specific) 2. SPRINT HEALTH: RAG status (Red/Amber/Green) with one-line justification 3. WHAT WE COMMITTED: Original plan (52 points, 15 stories) 4. WHERE WE ARE: Current completion rate and what's at risk 5. WHAT WE'RE DOING: The re-plan — what we're keeping, cutting, and why 6. ROOT CAUSE: Why is this sprint struggling? (reference the carry-over pattern from Sprint 13) 7. WHAT WE NEED: Any asks — decisions, unblocks, or support from leadership 8. NEXT SPRINT OUTLOOK: What we'll do differently in Sprint 15 Tone: transparent, accountable, forward-looking. Not defensive. Not panicky. The VP should finish reading and think "They have this under control." Keep it under 300 words.
6

Review & Iterate

Compare your outputs with your lab partner. Did Claude catch the same risks? Is the re-plan realistic? Does the stakeholder email strike the right tone?

Try asking Claude to adjust the tone (more formal, more casual), add more detail to a specific section, or rewrite the email for a different audience (e.g., the Product Owner instead of the VP).

Solution & Evaluation

Facilitator Guide

These are the expected outputs and evaluation criteria. Use these to guide discussion and assess participant results.

Expected Output — Velocity Analysis

Velocity Trend (Sprints 9–13)

  • Sprint 9: 32 completed
  • Sprint 10: 33 completed
  • Sprint 11: 30 completed
  • Sprint 12: 34 completed
  • Sprint 13: 26 completed

Average Velocity

~31 points over Sprints 9–13 ((32 + 33 + 30 + 34 + 26) / 5 = 31).

Over-Commitment

Sprint 14 was planned at 52 points — that's 68% above the average velocity of 31. This sprint was over-committed from day one, before a single story was started.

Remaining Capacity

With 3 days remaining out of a 2-week sprint (~30% of time left) and an average velocity of ~31 points, realistic remaining capacity is approximately 12–15 points. Combined with the 10 points already Done, the team can likely finish 22–25 total points.

Expected Output — Current Sprint Status

Points by Status

  • Done: 10 points (PROJ-309 = 2pts, plus other completed work)
  • In Progress: 16 points (PROJ-301 = 8pts, PROJ-302 = 3pts, PROJ-304 = 5pts)
  • To Do: 21 points (PROJ-303 = 5pts, PROJ-305 = 3pts, PROJ-306 = 8pts, PROJ-308 = 5pts)
  • Blocked: 5 points (PROJ-307 = 5pts)
  • Carry-Over (not started): varies by how the team has progressed on carry-over items

Completion Rate

Only 10/52 points Done (19%) with 3 days (approximately 30% of sprint time) remaining. The sprint is significantly behind pace.

Expected Output — Re-Plan Recommendations

KEEP (Realistic to complete)

  • PROJ-301 (SSO, 8pts) — Already In Progress, high priority, significant investment already made
  • PROJ-302 (Timeout bug, 3pts) — Critical bug, In Progress, small scope remaining
  • PROJ-304 (DB leak, 5pts) — Critical bug, In Progress, must-fix for reliability
  • PROJ-314 (Broken notifications, 2pts) — Carry-over, In Progress, small and high priority
  • PROJ-309 (CSV import fix, 2pts) — Already Done

CUT (Move to Sprint 15)

  • PROJ-303 (Pagination, 5pts) — Unassigned, medium priority, not started
  • PROJ-305 (API docs, 3pts) — Low priority, unassigned, not started
  • PROJ-306 (Dashboard views, 8pts) — 8 points, not started, too large for 3 days
  • PROJ-310 (Onboarding walkthrough, 5pts) — Low priority, unassigned
  • PROJ-311 (Perf test suite, 3pts) — Medium priority, unassigned, testing can wait
  • PROJ-313 (Schema migration, 8pts) — 8-point carry-over, risky migration for 3 days

DESCOPE (Deliver partial value)

  • PROJ-308 (Rate limiting, 5pts) — Implement basic throttling only, skip advanced configuration
  • PROJ-312 (Webhook retry, 5pts) — Implement basic retry logic, skip exponential backoff

CUT/UNBLOCK Decision Needed

  • PROJ-307 (Redis upgrade, 5pts) — Blocked. If cannot unblock today, cut to Sprint 15
  • PROJ-315 (Terraform module, 5pts) — Carry-over, cut to Sprint 15
Expected Output — Stakeholder Email

Key Elements to Look For

  • RAG Status: Amber (not Red — because the team has a plan; not Green — because they're significantly behind)
  • References the carry-over pattern from Sprint 13 as a root cause
  • Does NOT blame individuals — focuses on systemic issues (over-commitment, scope creep, carry-over debt)
  • Includes a concrete Sprint 15 commitment to plan at realistic velocity (~32–34 points)
  • Tone is transparent, accountable, and forward-looking
  • Under 300 words
Evaluation Checklist

Grade participant outputs against these criteria:

  1. Does the diagnosis show the math? — Velocity trend calculated, points broken down by status, percentages shown
  2. Is the over-commitment called out clearly? — 52 points vs. ~31 average velocity should be flagged as 68% over-commitment
  3. Is the re-plan realistic given the velocity data? — Revised target should be ~22–25 points, not an optimistic "we can still do 40"
  4. Does the stakeholder email tell the truth without causing panic? — Amber status, honest assessment, but with a concrete recovery plan
  5. Are Sprint 15 improvements mentioned? — Commit to realistic velocity, address carry-over debt, avoid repeating the pattern
