Hands-On Lab 1

The Interactive War Room

Build a live, interactive Decision Dashboard in 30 seconds using Claude Artifacts — no PowerBI, no analyst, no waiting.

Scenario

Crisis on the Dashboard

It's Monday morning. Your VP pings you: "Cloud costs spiked 15% last month. I need a breakdown by department on my desk in 30 minutes."

You don't have a PowerBI dashboard for this. Your data analyst is on PTO. All you have is a raw CSV export from your finance system.

The old way:

Open Excel. Build pivot tables. Format charts. Copy into PowerPoint. Email it. Elapsed time: 2-3 hours.

The AI way:

Upload the CSV to Claude. Describe what you need in one sentence. Get an interactive dashboard in 30 seconds.

The Problem

What You Need to Deliver

Using the sample data below (or your own budget/cost data), create:

  1. A department-level cost breakdown — which teams are over budget and by how much?
  2. An interactive toggle — the ability to switch between departments and see their individual spend vs. budget.
  3. A "What-If" analysis — what happens if leadership mandates a 5% across-the-board budget cut? Which departments can absorb it and which can't?
  4. A clear recommendation — in plain English, what should the VP do first?

Step-by-Step

How to Run This Lab

1

Open claude.ai

Go to claude.ai in your browser. Start a new conversation.

2

Upload the Data

Copy the table above into the chat, or drag and drop the sample-cloud-costs.csv file directly into Claude.

3

Prompt #1 — The Dashboard

Use the prompt below to generate the initial interactive dashboard.

Prompt #1 — Build the DashboardI just uploaded our March 2026 cloud cost report. I'm preparing for an emergency cost review with my VP in 30 minutes. Create an interactive dashboard as an Artifact that shows: 1. A bar chart comparing Budget vs. Actual spend for each department (aggregated). 2. A toggle or dropdown that lets me select a single department and see its service-level breakdown. 3. Color-code anything over budget in red and under budget in green. 4. Show the total company overspend at the top as a headline number. Make it clean, professional, and ready to screenshare in a meeting.
4

Prompt #2 — The "What-If" Scenario

Once the dashboard appears, ask for the scenario analysis.

Prompt #2 — What-If AnalysisNow add a "What-If" section to the dashboard: - Add a slider (or buttons) that lets me simulate a 5%, 10%, or 15% across-the-board budget cut. - For each scenario, show which departments would still be over their reduced budget. - Highlight the departments that would be hit hardest — i.e., where the gap between actual spend and the new reduced budget is largest. - Add a plain-English summary below the chart: "If you cut 5%, Engineering and Data Science will still be $X over the new target."
5

Prompt #3 — The Executive Recommendation

Ask Claude to turn the data into a decision memo.

Prompt #3 — Executive RecommendationBased on this analysis, write a one-page executive recommendation for my VP. Include: 1. Headline: The total overspend and the primary drivers. 2. Root causes: Why the top 3 over-budget items spiked (make reasonable inferences from the data). 3. Recommended actions: Rank-ordered by impact. Be specific — "Renegotiate LinkedIn Recruiter contract" not "reduce costs." 4. Timeline: What can be done this week vs. this quarter. Keep the tone direct and professional. No fluff. My VP reads fast.
6

Review, Iterate, Share

Check the dashboard and memo. Ask Claude to adjust anything: "Make the chart wider," "Add a pie chart for the department mix," "Rewrite the recommendation in bullet points." Share your result with your lab partner.

Solution Walkthrough

What Good Looks Like

Open the section below to see the expected outputs and what to look for.

Reveal the Solution & Evaluation Guide

Expected Dashboard Output

Claude should generate an Artifact containing an interactive HTML/JS dashboard. Here's what to verify:

  • Aggregated bar chart: Engineering ($75,200 actual vs. $65,500 budget), Data Science ($50,800 vs. $43,000), Marketing ($9,200 vs. $9,500), Sales ($22,500 vs. $22,200), HR ($16,200 vs. $14,500), Finance ($13,600 vs. $13,800), IT/Infra ($16,200 vs. $14,700)
  • Color coding: Red bars for Engineering (+$9,700), Data Science (+$7,800), HR (+$1,700), Sales (+$300), IT/Infra (+$1,500). Green for Marketing (-$300) and Finance (-$200).
  • Headline number: Total company overspend = approximately $18,800 (total actual ~$203,700 vs. total budget ~$183,200).
  • Department toggle: Selecting "Data Science" should show SageMaker at +$6,600 and Snowflake at +$1,200.

Expected What-If Output

The slider should show progressively tighter scenarios:

  • 5% cut: New total budget = ~$174,040. Engineering overshoot grows from $9,700 to $12,975. Data Science from $7,800 to $9,950. HR's LinkedIn Recruiter line remains the single most disproportionate item (+30.9%).
  • 10% cut: Even Marketing and Finance start showing red. Only fixed-price SaaS contracts (Salesforce, Workday, NetSuite) are "safe."
  • 15% cut: Nearly every department is over the reduced budget. This makes it clear a blanket cut won't work — targeted action is needed.

The plain-English summary should explicitly name the departments that can't absorb the cut.

Expected Executive Recommendation

A strong response will include something like:

Immediate Actions (This Week)

  • Audit SageMaker usage — likely orphaned training jobs or oversized instances (+$6,600 overspend = 23.6% over budget)
  • Review EC2 instance right-sizing in Engineering — $7,300 overspend suggests always-on instances that could be spot or reserved
  • Review LinkedIn Recruiter contract — 30.9% overspend on a $5,500 budget suggests seat count creep or tier mismatch

Quarterly Actions

  • Negotiate reserved instance pricing for EC2 and SageMaker (potential 30-40% savings)
  • Implement automated cost alerts at 90% budget threshold per department
  • Establish monthly cost review cadence (this dashboard can serve as the template)
  • Evaluate Azure AD licensing tier — 18.7% overspend may indicate a licensing mismatch

Evaluation Checklist

Score your output against these criteria:

  • Did the dashboard render as an interactive Artifact (not just text)?
  • Can you toggle between departments?
  • Does the What-If slider actually change the numbers and visuals?
  • Does the executive recommendation name specific services, not just departments?
  • Is the tone appropriate for a VP — direct, data-backed, no filler?
  • Could you screenshare this in a meeting right now and feel confident?
The "Wow" Realization

You just built a presentation-ready, interactive cost dashboard and a decision memo in under 5 minutes. No analyst. No PowerBI. No pivot tables. You described what you needed in plain English, and Claude delivered something you can act on immediately.

This is what it means to "talk your way to a visualization."
