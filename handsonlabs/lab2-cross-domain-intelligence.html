Hands-On Lab 2

Cross-Domain Intelligence

Use a Claude Project to connect the dots between HR, Legal, and Finance — and approve a vendor in minutes, not days.

Scenario

The Vendor That Needs Three Sign-Offs

A new vendor proposal just landed in your inbox. Before you can approve it, you need input from three different departments:

  • HR: Does the vendor's travel and expense policy align with our corporate policy?
  • Legal: Are the contract terms acceptable compared to our standard vendor agreement?
  • Finance: Does the pricing fit within our Q3 budget constraints?

Normally, this means three separate emails, three review cycles, and a 3-5 day wait before you get a green light.

With a Claude Project, you do it all in one conversation — in about 2 minutes.

Claude cross-references every document simultaneously and gives you a unified assessment.

The Problem

What You Need to Deliver

  1. Set up a Claude Project with three internal reference documents (HR policy, Legal standard terms, Finance budget constraints).
  2. Paste a new vendor proposal into the conversation.
  3. Get a single, unified review that checks the proposal against all three documents — flagging violations, risks, and budget mismatches.
  4. Generate a recommendation memo you can forward to leadership with a clear approve/reject/negotiate recommendation.

Step-by-Step

How to Run This Lab

1

Create a Claude Project

Go to claude.ai. Click "Projects" in the sidebar, then "New Project." Name it something like "Vendor Approvals."

2

Upload the Three Reference Documents

In the Project settings, upload (or paste) the three documents above: HR Travel Policy, Legal Standard Terms, and Finance Q3 Budget Constraints. These will be available in every conversation within this Project.

3

Set Project Instructions

In the Project's custom instructions, paste the following:

Project InstructionsYou are a vendor review assistant for Deltek. When I paste a vendor proposal, automatically cross-reference it against: 1. Our Corporate Travel & Expense Policy (HR) 2. Our Standard Vendor Agreement template (Legal) 3. Our Q3 2026 Budget Constraints memo (Finance) For each document, flag any violations, deviations, or risks. Use a clear Red / Yellow / Green rating for each area. Always end with a plain-English recommendation: Approve, Reject, or Negotiate — with specific terms to push back on.
4

Start a Conversation & Paste the Proposal

Open a new conversation inside the Project. Paste the Meridian Analytics proposal and add this prompt:

Prompt #1 — Cross-Domain ReviewHere's a new vendor proposal from Meridian Analytics. Please review it against all three of our internal documents. For each area (HR, Legal, Finance), give me: - A Red / Yellow / Green rating - Specific clauses or numbers that conflict with our standards - What we should push back on in negotiations Then give me a final recommendation: Approve as-is, Reject, or Approve with Conditions — and list the exact conditions.
5

Prompt #2 — Generate the Memo

Once you have the analysis, ask Claude to package it for leadership.

Prompt #2 — Decision MemoNow turn this analysis into a one-page decision memo I can send to my VP and the CFO. Include: 1. Vendor Summary: Who they are, what they offer, the cost. 2. Cross-Domain Assessment: A summary table with Red/Yellow/Green for HR, Legal, Finance. 3. Key Risks: The top 3 issues ranked by severity. 4. Recommended Negotiation Points: Exactly what to push back on, with suggested alternative terms. 5. Recommendation: One clear sentence. Keep it direct. The CFO doesn't read past page one.
6

Review, Iterate, Share

Check the analysis for accuracy. Ask Claude to adjust: "Make the legal section more detailed," or "Add a risk score." Share your output with your lab partner.

Solution Walkthrough

What Good Looks Like

Reveal the Solution & Evaluation Guide

Expected HR Assessment — YELLOW

  • Travel violation: Meridian offers to pay for business-class airfare directly. Deltek policy requires reimbursement only — vendor-paid airfare is prohibited. This needs restructuring.
  • Gift violation: The "Data Summit" conference pass is valued at $1,200 per attendee ($3,600 total for 3). This exceeds the $50 gift threshold by 24x and requires Ethics Committee disclosure at minimum. May need to be declined or reclassified as a training expense.
  • Hotel/meals: San Francisco is a Tier 1 city. 2-day on-site likely requires 2 nights — at the $225/night cap, this is fine as long as receipts are submitted within 30 days.

Expected Legal Assessment — RED

  • Auto-renewal: Proposal includes auto-renewal. Deltek standard terms explicitly prohibit this — all renewals must have explicit written consent. Must be removed.
  • Contract term: 2-year commitment. Deltek requires board approval for multi-year commitments. This is a blocker unless restructured as 1-year with option to renew.
  • Liability cap: 1x annual value ($42K). Deltek standard is 2x ($84K). This is a 50% gap — material risk exposure.
  • Indemnification gap: IP infringement is excluded. Deltek standard requires vendor to indemnify for both IP infringement and data breach. Must be added.
  • Early termination: 50% of remaining contract value as a fee, plus 90-day notice (vs. Deltek's 60-day standard). Extremely aggressive — could mean a $21,000 penalty.
  • Data residency: Singapore data center is outside US/EU. Deltek requires US or EU only. Must be restricted.
  • SOC 2 Type I vs. Type II: Deltek requires Type II. Type I is a point-in-time audit; Type II covers a period. This is a compliance gap.
  • Governing law: California vs. Deltek's Virginia standard. Negotiation point but not a blocker.
  • Payment terms: Net 30 vs. Deltek's Net 45. Minor but worth aligning.

Expected Finance Assessment — RED

  • Budget overshoot: $42,000/year exceeds the remaining Q3 headroom of $38,000 by $4,000. The contract cannot be approved under current Q3 limits without CFO exception.
  • CFO approval trigger: Any contract over $25,000 requires CFO sign-off. This will be triggered regardless.
  • Multi-year commitment: The 2-year term means $84,000 total commitment, which requires board approval per finance policy.
  • Payment preference mismatch: Meridian prefers annual upfront, but Deltek prefers quarterly payment terms. Negotiate for quarterly billing.

Expected Final Recommendation

Recommendation: Approve with Conditions (Negotiate First)

The service offering is relevant, but the proposal has 6 material issues that must be resolved before signing:

  1. Remove auto-renewal clause — replace with explicit annual renewal
  2. Restructure as 1-year contract to avoid board-approval requirement
  3. Increase liability cap to 2x annual value ($84K)
  4. Add IP infringement indemnification
  5. Restrict data processing to US/EU only — no Singapore
  6. Require SOC 2 Type II certification (or a committed timeline)
  7. Restructure onboarding travel as reimbursement, not vendor-paid
  8. Decline or reclassify the conference passes per ethics policy
  9. Negotiate to Net 45 and quarterly billing
  10. Reduce early termination penalty or remove entirely

Evaluation Checklist

  • Did Claude catch the auto-renewal prohibition?
  • Did it flag the data residency issue (Singapore)?
  • Did it identify the SOC 2 Type I vs. Type II gap?
  • Did it calculate the budget overshoot ($42K vs. $38K headroom)?
  • Did it flag the vendor-paid airfare as a travel policy violation?
  • Did it catch the conference pass gift exceeding the $50 threshold?
  • Did the memo include a clear Red/Yellow/Green rating for each domain?
  • Is the final recommendation specific enough to hand to a procurement team?

Key Takeaway

Why This Is a Game Changer

Before

You email HR, Legal, and Finance separately. Each takes 1-2 days to review. You aggregate their feedback manually. You write the summary yourself. Elapsed time: 3-5 business days.

After

You paste the proposal into a Project that already has your reference documents. Claude cross-references everything in one pass and generates a decision-ready memo. Elapsed time: 5 minutes.

This isn't about replacing HR, Legal, or Finance. It's about giving leaders a first-pass analysis so those teams can focus on the 20% that actually needs human judgment — not the 80% that's routine cross-referencing.
