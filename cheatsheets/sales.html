Prospecting & Outreach

Cold Outreach Email (Personalised)

Daily

Use when: Reaching out to a new prospect you've researched.

Write a cold outreach email to [Name, Title] at [Company]. What I know about them: - Company: [what they do, size, industry] - Recent news: [funding round, product launch, hiring spree, etc.] - Their likely pain point: [describe] What we offer: [one sentence about our product/service and the specific value] Rules: - Subject line under 8 words (write 3 options) - Email body under 100 words - Lead with THEIR problem, not our product - End with a low-friction CTA (not "Let me know when you're free for 30 minutes") - No buzzwords: no "synergy," "leverage," "innovative," "cutting-edge" - Tone: peer-to-peer, not salesy
Pro tip: Paste the prospect's LinkedIn profile or company "About" page to give Claude more context for personalisation.

Follow-Up Sequence (3-Touch)

Daily

Use when: Your initial outreach got no response and you need a follow-up cadence.

I sent a cold email to [Name at Company] about [topic] on [date]. No response. Write a 3-email follow-up sequence, spaced [3/5/7] days apart: 1. Follow-up 1: Short and different angle — don't just "bump" the thread. Add new value (a stat, a case study reference, a relevant insight). 2. Follow-up 2: Social proof approach — reference what similar companies in their industry have achieved. 3. Follow-up 3: Breakup email — gracious close that leaves the door open without being desperate. Each email: under 75 words. Different subject line for each. No guilt-tripping.

Account Research Brief

Per Deal

Use when: Preparing for a first call or meeting with a new account.

I have a first meeting with [Company Name] tomorrow. Here's their website: [URL or paste the About page] Give me a one-page account brief: 1. Company Overview: What they do, size, HQ, industry vertical 2. Recent News: Anything notable in the last 6 months (growth, funding, leadership changes, product launches) 3. Likely Pain Points: Based on their industry and size, what challenges are they probably facing that we can help with? 4. Key Questions to Ask: 5 smart discovery questions that show I've done my homework 5. Potential Objections: What they might push back on, and a one-line response for each Our product: [brief description of what we sell and to whom]

Deal Execution & Proposals

Proposal Executive Summary

Per Deal

Use when: Writing the executive summary section of a proposal or SOW.

Write the executive summary for a proposal to [Client Name]. Their challenge: [describe the problem they shared during discovery] Our solution: [describe what we're proposing] Key outcomes: [list 2-3 expected results with metrics if possible] Timeline: [implementation timeline] Investment: [$X] Rules: - Under 250 words - Lead with THEIR problem and desired outcome, not our features - Include one quantified benefit ("reduce X by Y%" or "save Z hours/week") - Close with a clear next step - Tone: confident and consultative, not promotional

Competitive Positioning (Battlecard)

Per Deal

Use when: You know you're up against a specific competitor in a deal.

I'm competing against [Competitor Name] for a deal with [Client]. What I know about the competitor: [Paste any info: their website, pricing page, reviews, or "I don't know much"] What the client cares about most: [List their top 2-3 priorities from discovery] Create a battlecard: 1. Where We Win: 3 areas where we're genuinely stronger (with proof points) 2. Where They Win: 3 areas where they might have an edge (be honest) 3. How to Neutralise: For each area they win, a talking point to minimize the gap 4. Landmine Questions: 3 questions I can plant that make the client think twice about choosing them 5. Trap to Avoid: What the competitor might say about us, and how to pre-empt it Be honest, not delusional. I'd rather know the truth than lose the deal unprepared.

Objection Handler

Daily

Use when: Preparing for a call where you expect pushback, or debriefing after one.

The prospect said: "[Paste the exact objection, e.g., 'We're happy with our current vendor', 'The price is too high', 'We don't have bandwidth right now']" Context: [deal stage, what's been discussed, what we know about their situation] Give me: 1. What they really mean: Decode the underlying concern (it's rarely what they literally said) 2. Acknowledge response: A sentence that validates their concern without caving 3. Reframe response: A sentence that shifts the conversation to value 4. Question to ask next: A follow-up question that keeps the conversation moving 5. If they push back again: A graceful "next step" that preserves the relationship Tone: consultative, not defensive. Never argue with a prospect.

Pipeline & Forecasting

Pipeline Health Check

Weekly

Use when: Reviewing your pipeline before a forecast call or 1:1 with your manager.

Here's my current pipeline: [Paste or upload deal list with: Deal Name, Stage, Amount, Close Date, Last Activity Date, Next Step] Analyse for: 1. Stale deals: Anything with no activity in 14+ days — suggest an action for each 2. Close date risk: Deals with a close date in the next 30 days that are still in early stages 3. Stage distribution: Am I top-heavy (too much in early stages) or bottom-heavy (not enough new pipeline)? 4. Revenue at risk: Total $ in deals that have at least one red flag 5. Forecast confidence: Based on the data, what's a realistic number I should commit vs. what I should call "best case"? Give me the output as a table + a 3-sentence summary I can say on the forecast call.

Deal Strategy (Complex / Enterprise)

Per Deal

Use when: Planning strategy for a high-value, multi-stakeholder deal.

Help me build a deal strategy for [Company Name]. Deal size: [$X] Current stage: [Discovery / Demo / Proposal / Negotiation] Decision timeline: [when they need to decide] Champion: [name, title, what they care about] Economic buyer: [name, title, what they care about] Other stakeholders: [list with their roles and concerns] Competition: [who else is in the deal, if known] Biggest risk: [what could kill this deal] Give me: 1. A stakeholder influence map (who do we need to win, who could block us) 2. A next-steps plan for the next 2 weeks (specific actions, not generic) 3. The "must-win" moments — the 2-3 interactions that will make or break this deal 4. A "deal-killer" checklist — signals that this deal is going sideways

Win/Loss Analysis

As Needed

Use when: A deal closed (won or lost) and you want to extract lessons.

Analyse this deal outcome: Deal: [Company Name] — [Won / Lost] Deal size: [$X] Sales cycle: [X weeks/months] Competitor: [who we were up against, if applicable] What happened: [Chronological summary: first contact, key meetings, proposal, what the client said, final decision] Give me: 1. What worked: 2-3 things we did right (be specific) 2. What didn't: 2-3 things we could improve (be honest) 3. Key moment: The single interaction or decision that most influenced the outcome 4. Pattern to watch: Based on this deal, is there a pattern I should look for in future deals? 5. One-line lesson: If I could tell my past self one thing before this deal started, what would it be?
