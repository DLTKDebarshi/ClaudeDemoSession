Team Lead

Lead your people, not just your projects. Prompts for 1:1s, hiring, performance, team health, and the human side of engineering.

💬 1:1 Meetings & Coaching

1:1 Meeting Prep & Questions

Weekly

Generate a structured 1:1 agenda with context-aware questions based on the person's current situation, recent work, and career goals.

I'm preparing for a 1:1 with [person's name], a [role/title] on my team. Here's their context: - Currently working on: [project or initiative] - Recent wins: [specific accomplishment or milestone] - Current challenges: [blockers, frustrations, or concerns you've noticed] - Career goal: [what they've expressed wanting to grow toward] - Last 1:1 action items: [any follow-ups from previous meeting] Generate a structured 1:1 agenda with: 1. An opening check-in question (not "how are you?") 2. 2-3 questions about their current work and blockers 3. 1 question connecting their work to their career growth 4. A follow-up on last meeting's action items 5. A closing question that surfaces anything I might be missing Keep the tone supportive and coaching-oriented, not status-report-driven.
Pro Tip: Keep a running doc per person. Paste their previous 1:1 notes into context for even more personalised questions over time.

Difficult Conversation Prep

As Needed

Plan and script a sensitive conversation (underperformance, conflict, unwanted news) with empathetic framing and clear expectations.

I need to have a difficult conversation with [person's name] ([their role]) about [topic: e.g., missed deadlines / interpersonal conflict / role change / PIP]. Context: - Specific examples of the issue: [2-3 concrete, dated instances] - Impact on team/project: [how this is affecting others or delivery] - Previous feedback given: [any prior conversations or written feedback] - Desired outcome: [what good looks like going forward] - Their likely perspective: [how they might see the situation] Help me prepare: 1. An opening statement that is direct but empathetic (no "sandwich" feedback) 2. A clear description of the issue using specific examples 3. Active listening prompts to understand their perspective 4. A collaborative section where we agree on next steps together 5. A summary of expectations and a timeline for follow-up 6. Contingency responses if they become defensive or emotional Keep the tone respectful, factual, and forward-looking.
Pro Tip: Practice reading the script aloud. If any sentence feels unnatural, rewrite it in your own words -- authenticity matters more than polish.

Career Development Discussion

Quarterly

Structure a career growth conversation covering current skills, aspirations, gap analysis, and a concrete action plan.

I'm preparing a career development discussion with [person's name], currently a [current role/level] on my team. Their profile: - Time in current role: [duration] - Key strengths: [2-3 demonstrated strengths] - Areas for growth: [skills or behaviours to develop] - Expressed career interest: [where they want to go -- e.g., senior IC, management, specialisation] - Company level framework: [link or description of your engineering ladder, if applicable] Create a structured career conversation plan: 1. Opening: reflection questions for them to self-assess where they are 2. Gap analysis: map their current skills against the next level's expectations 3. Opportunities: 3-5 specific stretch assignments, projects, or learning paths that close the gaps 4. Timeline: a realistic quarter-by-quarter roadmap 5. Support: what I can do as their manager to unblock their growth 6. Success metrics: how we'll both know they're making progress Frame everything as collaborative -- this is their career, I'm the enabler.
🔍 Hiring & Onboarding

Hiring Scorecard & Interview Plan

Per Hire

Design a structured interview process with competency-based scorecards for a specific role on your team.

I'm hiring a [role title] for my team. Here's the context: - Team focus: [what the team builds/owns] - Key technologies: [tech stack the person will work with] - Seniority level: [junior / mid / senior / staff] - Must-have skills: [3-5 non-negotiable competencies] - Nice-to-have skills: [2-3 bonus areas] - Team culture values: [e.g., ownership, collaboration, candour] Design a complete interview plan: 1. A hiring scorecard with 6-8 competencies, each with a 1-5 rating scale and behavioral anchors 2. Interview stages (screen, technical, system design, team fit) with who should run each 3. 2-3 specific questions per stage tied to scorecard competencies 4. A take-home or live exercise option with clear evaluation criteria 5. Red flags and green flags to watch for at each stage 6. A debrief template for the hiring panel to fill out Optimise for consistency and reducing unconscious bias.
Pro Tip: Share the scorecard with all interviewers before the loop starts. Calibration upfront eliminates "gut feel" debates in the debrief.

New Team Member Onboarding Plan

Per Hire

Create a week-by-week onboarding plan tailored to the role, including buddy assignments, learning milestones, and first deliverable.

A new [role title] is joining my team on [start date]. Help me build their onboarding plan. Context: - Their background: [relevant experience, where they're coming from] - Team they're joining: [team name, size, what you own] - Key systems they'll work with: [repos, services, tools] - Their first project: [the initial deliverable or area of focus] - Assigned buddy: [name and role of their onboarding buddy] Create a 30-60-90 day onboarding plan: Week 1-2 (Orientation): - Day-by-day schedule with meetings, reading, and setup tasks - Key people to meet and why Week 3-4 (Learning): - First small contributions (e.g., bug fix, documentation) - Systems deep-dives to attend or shadow Month 2 (Contributing): - First meaningful deliverable with clear scope - Checkpoint: what "on track" looks like at day 60 Month 3 (Owning): - Independent ownership of an area - 90-day review criteria Include a checklist format I can share with them on day one.

Headcount Justification

As Needed

Build a data-backed proposal for adding headcount with workload analysis, impact projection, and hiring timeline.

I need to justify adding [number] headcount to my team for [quarter/half/year]. Help me build a compelling business case. Current state: - Team size: [current number] engineers - Current workload: [list active projects and maintenance areas] - Utilisation signals: [overtime trends, sprint spillover %, on-call burden, etc.] - What's being dropped or delayed: [projects on hold, tech debt accumulating, SLA risks] Future demand: - Upcoming commitments: [roadmap items, new product features, migrations] - Strategic initiatives: [things leadership wants that you can't staff today] Build a headcount proposal with: 1. Executive summary (3 sentences max) 2. Workload analysis: current capacity vs demand, with data 3. Risk of NOT hiring: concrete impacts on delivery, reliability, retention 4. Role specifications: what each new hire would own 5. Expected ROI: what becomes possible with additional capacity 6. Hiring timeline: realistic ramp-up and time-to-productivity 7. Alternative considered: why contractors, re-org, or tooling alone won't solve this Format for a VP-level audience -- concise, data-driven, no fluff.
Pro Tip: Anchor your ask to revenue or reliability metrics your VP already tracks. "We need help" is a feeling; "we'll miss the Q3 launch" is a business risk.
📈 Performance & Growth

Performance Review Writer

Quarterly

Synthesise feedback from multiple sources into a balanced, actionable performance review with clear ratings and growth areas.

Help me write a performance review for [person's name], [role/level], for the [Q1 2026 / H2 2025 / etc.] review cycle. Input data: - Self-assessment summary: [paste or summarise their self-review] - Peer feedback themes: [key points from 2-3 peer reviews] - My observations: [specific examples of impact, both positive and developmental] - Key deliverables this period: [what they shipped or accomplished] - Goals from last review: [what they committed to and progress made] - Level expectations: [what's expected at their current level] Write a performance review with: 1. Overall rating recommendation with justification (1 paragraph) 2. Strengths (2-3) with specific evidence and impact 3. Growth areas (1-2) with specific examples and concrete suggestions 4. Progress on previous goals (met / partially met / not met with context) 5. Goals for next period (2-3 SMART goals) 6. Summary statement suitable for the official review record Tone: honest, specific, supportive. Avoid generic praise ("great team player") -- always tie to observable behaviour and outcomes.

Team Skills Matrix

Quarterly

Map team capabilities against required competencies, identify gaps, and plan cross-training or hiring to fill them.

Help me build a skills matrix for my team to identify capability gaps and plan development. Team members and their primary strengths: [Name 1] - [key skills, e.g., backend Java, system design, mentoring] [Name 2] - [key skills] [Name 3] - [key skills] [... add all team members] Competencies we need to cover: [List required skills: e.g., Kubernetes, CI/CD, frontend React, database tuning, incident response, security, technical writing] Create: 1. A skills matrix table (team members as rows, competencies as columns) with ratings: Expert / Proficient / Learning / Gap 2. Bus factor analysis: competencies where only 1 person is proficient or above 3. Critical gaps: competencies with no one at proficient level 4. A cross-training plan: who should learn what from whom, with suggested format (pairing, shadowing, courses) 5. Hiring implications: gaps that are better filled by hiring than training 6. A quarterly check-in template to reassess progress Output the matrix in a format I can paste into a spreadsheet.
Pro Tip: Let team members self-rate first, then calibrate together. The conversation about the matrix is often more valuable than the matrix itself.

Recognition & Shout-Outs

Weekly

Draft specific, meaningful recognition messages that highlight impact rather than just activity.

Help me write a recognition message for [person's name] to share in [Slack channel / team meeting / email to leadership / review doc]. What they did: [specific action or contribution] Context that makes it impressive: [why this was hard, what constraints they overcame] Impact: [measurable outcome, unblocked team, improved reliability, etc.] Values demonstrated: [e.g., ownership, collaboration, going above and beyond] Write 3 versions: 1. A short Slack shout-out (2-3 sentences, team channel appropriate) 2. A more detailed message suitable for sharing with their skip-level or in a review doc (1 paragraph) 3. A nomination-style write-up if I wanted to put them forward for a company award (3-4 sentences with clear impact statement) Make each version specific to what THEY did -- avoid generic praise. Highlight impact over effort.
Pro Tip: Timely recognition beats perfect recognition. Send the Slack version today; save the detailed version for their review file.
🌱 Team Health & Culture

Team Health Assessment

Monthly

Design and analyse a team health survey covering psychological safety, workload, collaboration, and clarity of direction.

Help me create and analyse a team health check for my [team name] team ([team size] people). Current context: - Recent events: [reorg, launch, attrition, new members, etc.] - Known concerns: [anything you've heard informally] - Last health check results (if any): [previous scores or themes] Create: 1. A team health survey with 8-10 statements rated on Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree, covering: - Psychological safety ("I feel safe raising concerns") - Workload sustainability - Clarity of goals and priorities - Collaboration and communication - Manager support and feedback - Growth and learning opportunities - Tooling and process efficiency - Overall team morale 2. 2-3 open-ended questions to surface qualitative insights 3. An analysis framework: how to interpret results, identify patterns, and prioritise action 4. An action plan template: for each low-scoring area, suggest a concrete intervention 5. A communication template for sharing results transparently with the team Keep the survey anonymous-friendly and under 5 minutes to complete.

Conflict Resolution Framework

As Needed

Structure an approach to resolve interpersonal or cross-team conflict with mediation steps and documented agreements.

I need to mediate a conflict on my team. Help me structure my approach. Situation: - People involved: [Person A role] and [Person B role] - Nature of conflict: [technical disagreement / communication style clash / workload dispute / role boundary confusion] - Specific incidents: [1-2 concrete examples of the friction] - Impact on team: [how this is affecting collaboration, delivery, or morale] - What I've tried so far: [any previous interventions] Create a conflict resolution plan: 1. Pre-mediation: individual conversations with each person (questions to ask, what to listen for) 2. Joint session structure: ground rules, agenda, and facilitation guide 3. Root cause exploration: questions that surface underlying needs, not just positions 4. Agreement framework: a template for documenting what both parties commit to 5. Follow-up cadence: how and when to check that the resolution is holding 6. Escalation criteria: when to involve HR, skip-level, or consider team changes Keep the approach neutral, focused on behaviour not personality, and solution-oriented.
Pro Tip: Talk to each person individually first. Never ambush someone with a mediation session they didn't know was coming.

Team Charter / Working Agreement

Per Team

Facilitate creation of a team charter covering values, communication norms, decision-making process, and meeting cadence.

Help me facilitate the creation of a team charter for my [team name] team. Team context: - Team size: [number] people - Distribution: [co-located / hybrid / fully remote, time zones] - Team maturity: [newly formed / recently changed / stable and established] - Primary tools: [Slack, Jira, Confluence, GitHub, etc.] - Pain points to address: [e.g., unclear ownership, meeting overload, slow decisions] Create a team charter template and facilitation guide covering: 1. Team Mission: a 1-2 sentence purpose statement (draft 3 options for the team to vote on) 2. Communication Norms: - When to use Slack vs email vs meeting - Expected response times by channel - Core overlap hours for the team 3. Decision-Making: - Who decides what (RACI-style) - How to handle disagreements (e.g., disagree-and-commit, escalation path) 4. Meeting Cadence: - Required vs optional meetings - Meeting hygiene rules (agendas, notes, no-meeting blocks) 5. Code of Conduct: - How we give feedback - How we handle mistakes - How we celebrate wins 6. A facilitation agenda for a 60-minute workshop where the team co-creates this charter together Format as a living document the team can iterate on quarterly.
Pro Tip: Don't write this alone and present it. The charter only works if the team co-creates it. Use the facilitation guide to run the session together.
