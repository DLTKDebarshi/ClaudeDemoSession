Daily Operations

Expense Categorisation & Anomaly Check

Daily

Use when: Processing expense reports or reviewing credit card transactions.

Here are this week's expense transactions. Please: 1. Categorise each line item into our standard GL categories: [Travel, Software, Meals, Office Supplies, Professional Services, Other] 2. Flag any transaction that looks unusual — duplicates, round-number amounts over $500, weekend charges, or vendors we haven't seen before 3. For flagged items, explain WHY it's unusual in one sentence 4. Give me a category-level subtotal at the bottom Output as a clean table I can paste into a spreadsheet. [Paste your transaction data here]
Pro tip: Upload the CSV directly instead of pasting — Claude handles file attachments and keeps the formatting clean.

Journal Entry Review

Daily

Use when: Checking journal entries before posting to the ledger.

Review these journal entries for errors before I post them: 1. Verify that debits equal credits for each entry 2. Flag any accounts that seem unusual for the transaction type (e.g., revenue booked to an expense account) 3. Check for missing descriptions or reference numbers 4. Identify any entries over [$X,000] that may need manager approval per our policy [Paste journal entries here]

Invoice Discrepancy Checker

Daily

Use when: Matching invoices against POs or contracts.

Compare this invoice against the original purchase order: [Paste or describe the PO details: vendor, line items, agreed rates, total] [Paste or describe the invoice details] Flag: - Any line item where the invoiced amount exceeds the PO amount - Missing or extra line items - Rate differences (even small ones) - Tax calculation errors - Payment terms that don't match the PO Give me a summary table: PO Amount | Invoiced Amount | Difference | Issue

Weekly Reviews

Cash Flow Summary

Weekly

Use when: Preparing the weekly cash position report for leadership.

Here's our bank balance and this week's AR/AP activity: Opening balance: [$X] [Paste receivables collected, payables paid, and pending items] Please create: 1. A clean cash flow summary: Opening → Inflows → Outflows → Closing Balance 2. A 2-week forward look based on known receivables due and payables scheduled 3. Flag any week where projected cash drops below [$X minimum threshold] 4. List the top 5 outstanding receivables by amount and days overdue Keep it to one page — my CFO reads this in 2 minutes.

Vendor Spend Analysis

Weekly

Use when: Reviewing vendor spend for cost control meetings.

Analyse this vendor spend data for [month/quarter]: [Paste or upload vendor payment data] I need: 1. Top 10 vendors by total spend 2. Any vendor where spend increased more than 15% vs. the prior period 3. Vendors we're paying but have no active contract on file (flag for procurement review): [list known contracted vendors] 4. Potential consolidation opportunities — are we paying multiple vendors for similar services? Format as a table with a brief narrative summary I can present at our Thursday cost review.

Month-End & Period Close

P&L Variance Analysis

Monthly

Use when: Comparing actuals against budget after month-end close.

You are a financial analyst at a professional services firm. Here are two datasets: 1. [Month] Budget (planned) 2. [Month] Actuals [Paste or upload both datasets] Please: 1. Create a variance report: Budget | Actual | $ Variance | % Variance for each line item 2. Highlight anything with a variance greater than [5%/$5,000] 3. For the top 5 largest variances, write a one-sentence plain-English explanation of the likely cause 4. Summarise the overall P&L health in 3 bullets a CFO can read in 30 seconds Do NOT use jargon — explain as if the reader is a non-finance executive.
Pro tip: Upload the budget and actuals as separate CSV files for the cleanest comparison. Say "Create a chart of budget vs. actual by category" to get a visual Artifact.

Month-End Close Checklist Generator

Monthly

Use when: Kicking off month-end close to make sure nothing falls through the cracks.

Generate a month-end close checklist for [Month Year] for a [company type: e.g., mid-market SaaS / professional services / construction] company. Include: 1. Pre-close tasks (cut-off procedures, accrual reviews) 2. Close tasks (reconciliations, journal entries, intercompany eliminations) 3. Post-close tasks (variance analysis, reporting, sign-offs) 4. Estimated time per task 5. Owner column (leave blank — I'll fill in) Special items this month: [e.g., "We had a large asset purchase", "New revenue recognition for a multi-year contract", "Headcount change mid-month"] Format as a table I can paste into a project tracker.

Account Reconciliation Helper

Monthly

Use when: Reconciling GL accounts against bank statements or sub-ledgers.

Help me reconcile [account name/number]. GL balance as of [date]: [$X] Bank/sub-ledger balance: [$Y] Difference: [$Z] Here are the transactions from both sources: [Paste GL transactions] [Paste bank/sub-ledger transactions] Please: 1. Match transactions between both sources (by amount, date, and description) 2. List all unmatched items from each source 3. Categorise unmatched items: timing differences, missing entries, or potential errors 4. Suggest a reconciling journal entry if needed

Reporting & Forecasting

Budget Forecast Update

Monthly

Use when: Updating the rolling forecast based on actuals-to-date.

Here are our YTD actuals through [Month] and the original annual budget: [Paste or upload data] Please: 1. Calculate the run-rate for each category based on YTD actuals 2. Project the full-year estimate using the run-rate 3. Compare the projection against the original budget 4. Flag any category where we're trending more than [10%] over or under budget 5. Provide a one-paragraph narrative: "Based on current trends, we expect to finish the year at $X against a budget of $Y, a variance of Z%. The primary drivers are..." Make it presentation-ready for a board meeting.

Financial Narrative for Leadership

Monthly

Use when: Translating raw numbers into the "story" for non-finance executives.

Here's our [month/quarter] financial summary: [Paste key metrics: revenue, COGS, gross margin, opex, EBITDA, cash, headcount] Write a 3-paragraph financial narrative for our leadership team: - Paragraph 1: The headline — how did we do vs. plan? One sentence, clear verdict. - Paragraph 2: What drove the result — the 2-3 biggest movers (positive and negative). - Paragraph 3: What to watch — risks or opportunities for next period. Tone: confident, direct, no hedging. Write like a CFO briefing a CEO, not like a textbook.

Audit Preparation Summary

As Needed

Use when: Preparing documentation for an internal or external audit.

We have an upcoming [internal/external] audit for [period] focusing on [area: e.g., revenue recognition, expense controls, fixed assets]. Please help me prepare: 1. A checklist of documents the auditors will likely request 2. Key controls they'll want to test and what evidence we need 3. Common audit findings in this area and how to pre-emptively address them 4. A timeline working backward from the audit start date of [date] Our company is a [type/size] using [accounting framework: US GAAP / IFRS]. Format as a structured prep guide I can share with my team.
