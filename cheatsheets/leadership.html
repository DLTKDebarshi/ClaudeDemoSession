Leadership & Strategy

Reclaim the hours you spend aggregating, summarising, and formatting. Lead with the output, not the busywork.

📚

Synthesis & Summarisation

Meeting Transcript → Executive Memo

Daily

Use when: You just left a long meeting and need a clean summary with owners and deadlines.

Here's a transcript (or my raw notes) from a [type of meeting: leadership sync / project review / strategy session]. [Paste transcript or notes] Turn this into a one-page Executive Memo with: 1. Key Decisions Made — what was agreed, in one line each 2. Action Items — table format: Action | Owner | Deadline 3. Open Questions — things that weren't resolved 4. Parking Lot — topics raised but deferred Tone: direct and professional. No filler. Each section should be scannable in 15 seconds.
Pro tip: If you record meetings, upload the audio file directly to Claude — it will transcribe and summarise in one step.

Long Document → Executive Summary

Daily

Use when: Someone sent you a 20-page report and you need the gist before a 2 PM meeting.

Summarise this document into an executive brief for a [VP / C-suite / board member]: [Paste or upload the document] Structure: - Bottom Line Up Front: One sentence — the single most important takeaway. - Key Findings: 3-5 bullet points, each one sentence. - Implications for Us: What does this mean for our [team/project/company]? - Recommended Next Step: One concrete action. Hard limit: 300 words. My reader has 90 seconds.

Weekly Status Report Generator

Weekly

Use when: It's Friday and you need to send your VP an update.

Here are my raw notes from this week: [Paste bullet points, Slack messages, task updates, or just brain-dump] Turn this into a professional weekly status report: - Accomplishments: What got done (results, not activities) - In Progress: What's underway with expected completion - Blockers / Risks: Anything that needs leadership attention - Next Week Focus: Top 3 priorities Keep each bullet to one line. My VP reads 15 of these every Friday — make mine the easiest to scan.

Decision Making & Analysis

Decision Memo (Options Analysis)

Weekly

Use when: You need to present options to a decision-maker with a clear recommendation.

I need to make a decision about [describe the decision]. Context: [Background — why this decision matters, timeline, constraints] The options are: - Option A: [describe] - Option B: [describe] - Option C: [describe, or "Status quo"] For each option, give me: 1. Pros (2-3 bullets) 2. Cons (2-3 bullets) 3. Estimated cost / effort 4. Risk level (Low / Medium / High) with a one-line justification Then give me your recommendation and explain why in 2-3 sentences. Format this as a decision memo I can share with [audience: my VP / the leadership team / the board].

Stakeholder Impact Analysis

As Needed

Use when: Planning a change that affects multiple teams or stakeholders.

We're planning to [describe the change: reorg, new tool rollout, policy change, process update]. The stakeholders involved are: [List groups: e.g., Engineering, Sales, Finance, External Partners, Customers] For each stakeholder group, analyse: 1. How are they impacted? (High / Medium / Low) 2. What changes for them specifically? 3. What's their likely reaction? (Supportive / Neutral / Resistant) 4. What do we need to communicate to them, and when? 5. Who should deliver that message? Format as a stakeholder map table, then give me a recommended communication sequence (who gets told first, second, etc.) with rationale.

Pre-Mortem (Risk Identification)

As Needed

Use when: About to kick off a major initiative and want to anticipate what could go wrong.

We're about to launch [initiative/project description]. Timeline: [X weeks/months] Team: [team size and composition] Budget: [if relevant] Key dependencies: [list any] Run a pre-mortem: Imagine it's [end date] and this project has failed. What went wrong? Give me: 1. Top 10 failure modes, ranked by likelihood x impact 2. For each: a one-line description, the early warning sign, and one preventive action 3. The 3 risks I should be watching personally as the leader Be brutally honest. I'd rather hear it now than in a post-mortem.

Communication & Announcements

Org Change Announcement

As Needed

Use when: Communicating a reorg, leadership change, or structural update.

Draft an announcement email for the following change: [Describe the change: new team structure, role changes, reporting line updates] Constraints: - Tone: [warm and direct / formal / casual but clear] - DO mention: [list what to include] - DO NOT mention: [list sensitive items to exclude] - Length: under [300/400/500] words - Sign from: [name and title] The audience is [who will read this]. They care most about: "How does this affect me?" End with a clear "what happens next" section and how to ask questions.

Board / Executive Presentation Outline

Monthly

Use when: Preparing a presentation for the board, executive committee, or senior leadership.

I'm presenting to [audience: board / exec team / investors] on [topic]. Time slot: [X minutes] Goal: [inform / get approval / align on direction] Key data points I have: [List 5-8 facts, metrics, or decisions you want to cover] Create: 1. A slide-by-slide outline (title + 2-3 bullet points per slide) 2. An "anticipated questions" section with suggested answers 3. A one-sentence opening that hooks the audience immediately 4. A closing slide with a clear ask or next step Rule: No slide should have more than 3 bullets. If I can't present each slide in 90 seconds, it's too dense.

OKR / Goal Drafting

Quarterly

Use when: Setting quarterly or annual goals for your team.

Help me draft OKRs for [team/department name] for [Q3 2026 / H2 2026 / FY2027]. Our company-level priorities are: [List 2-3 company objectives] My team's mandate: [describe what the team does and its strategic role] Last quarter's results: [Brief summary of what was achieved and what was missed] Draft 3 Objectives, each with 2-3 Key Results. Each Key Result should be: - Measurable (specific number or clear yes/no) - Ambitious but achievable (not sandbagged, not fantasy) - Within this team's control Then add a "What We Will NOT Do" section — 2-3 things we're explicitly deprioritising. This is as important as the goals themselves.

Resource Planning & Staffing Proposal

As Needed

Use when: Building a case for headcount, contractors, or budget to support a project or growth.

I need to build a resource plan for [project/initiative]. Scope: [describe what needs to happen] Timeline: [start to finish] Current team: [who's available and their capacity] Please: 1. Break the work into phases with key milestones 2. Identify the roles needed for each phase (title, FTE %, duration) 3. Flag gaps between what I have and what I need 4. Estimate the cost of filling those gaps (use [$X/hr for contractors, $Y salary for FTEs]) 5. Create a one-page staffing proposal I can send to my VP with a clear ask Format: executive-ready, not a project plan. Keep it to one page with a table.
