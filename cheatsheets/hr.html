Recruiting & Hiring

Job Description Writer

Per Hire

Use when: Opening a new req and need a compelling, unbiased JD fast.

Write a job description for: [Job Title] Department: [team name] Reports to: [manager title] Level: [junior / mid / senior / lead] Location: [remote / hybrid / office city] Key responsibilities (my rough notes): [Brain-dump the main duties] Must-have skills: [list hard requirements] Nice-to-have: [list preferred but not required] Rules: - Use inclusive, gender-neutral language throughout - Avoid "rockstar," "ninja," "guru," or other exclusionary terms - Separate "requirements" from "nice-to-haves" clearly (research shows underrepresented groups self-select out when lists are blurred) - Include a 30/60/90-day snapshot: what does success look like in the first 3 months? - Keep it under 500 words — longer JDs get fewer applicants - End with our commitment to diversity statement: [paste yours, or ask me to draft one]
Pro tip: Ask Claude to "Review this JD for bias" after generating it — it will flag terms that may discourage diverse applicants.

Interview Question Generator

Per Hire

Use when: Preparing interview guides for a hiring panel.

Generate a structured interview guide for: [Job Title] Interview stage: [phone screen / technical / behavioral / final round] Interviewer: [role of the person conducting: hiring manager / peer / skip-level] Duration: [30 / 45 / 60 minutes] Key competencies to assess: [List 3-5: e.g., problem-solving, cross-functional collaboration, data-driven decision-making, customer empathy] For each competency, give me: 1. A behavioral question (STAR format: "Tell me about a time when...") 2. A follow-up probe (to dig deeper into their answer) 3. What a STRONG answer sounds like (key signals to listen for) 4. What a WEAK answer sounds like (red flags) Also include: - 2 opening rapport-building questions - A closing "What questions do you have for us?" framework - A quick scorecard template the interviewer can fill out in 2 minutes after the call

Offer Letter / Rejection Email

Per Hire

Use when: You've made a decision and need to communicate it well.

Draft a [offer letter / rejection email] for [Candidate Name] who interviewed for [Job Title]. [If offer:] - Compensation: [base, bonus, equity if applicable] - Start date: [date] - Key selling points about the role/team: [list 2-3] - Tone: excited and warm — make them feel chosen, not processed [If rejection:] - Reason (internal, do not state explicitly): [e.g., another candidate had more relevant experience] - Tone: respectful, genuine, and encouraging - Leave the door open for future roles if they were a strong candidate - Do NOT give specific feedback on weaknesses (legal risk) - Keep it under 150 words — long rejections feel worse

Performance & Development

Performance Review Synthesis

Quarterly / Annual

Use when: Writing or preparing for performance reviews from multiple data sources.

I need to write a performance review for [Employee Name, Title]. Here's the raw input: - Self-assessment: [paste or summarise] - Peer feedback (3 sources): [paste or summarise] - Manager notes: [paste your observations] - Key metrics/outcomes: [deliverables, projects, numbers] Write a balanced performance review that includes: 1. Summary: 2-3 sentences overall assessment 2. Strengths: 3 specific strengths with evidence from the input 3. Growth Areas: 2 areas for development with specific, actionable suggestions (not vague "improve communication") 4. Key Achievements: Top 3 accomplishments this period 5. Development Plan: 2-3 concrete next steps with suggested timeline Tone: honest, supportive, growth-oriented. Avoid hedging language ("sort of," "kind of"). Be direct but kind.
Pro tip: Upload multiple peer feedback forms at once. Ask Claude to "Identify consistent themes across all feedback sources" before writing the review.

90-Day Development Plan

Per Employee

Use when: An employee needs a structured development plan after a review or promotion.

Create a 90-day development plan for [Employee Name, Title]. Current performance level: [meets expectations / exceeds / needs improvement] Growth areas identified: [list 2-3 from their review] Career aspiration: [where they want to grow: management track, technical depth, cross-functional] Available resources: [budget for training, mentorship available, stretch projects, etc.] Structure the plan in 30-day blocks: - Days 1-30: Foundation — awareness and learning - Days 31-60: Application — practice in real work - Days 61-90: Demonstration — visible impact For each block include: 1. Specific actions (not "read more about leadership") 2. Success criteria (how do we know this is working?) 3. Check-in cadence with their manager 4. One stretch assignment that pushes them outside their comfort zone

PIP (Performance Improvement Plan) Draft

As Needed

Use when: An employee needs formal documented improvement expectations. Always review with legal.

Draft a Performance Improvement Plan for an employee in a [role type] position. Performance gaps: [List specific, documented issues with dates and examples] Previous coaching provided: [List prior conversations, feedback, and support given] Draft a PIP that includes: 1. Specific expectations: What does "meets expectations" look like? (measurable) 2. Support provided: What the company will do to help (training, mentoring, reduced workload) 3. Timeline: [30/60/90 days] with check-in dates 4. Success metrics: Clear, objective criteria for each expectation 5. Consequences: What happens if expectations are not met Tone: factual, fair, and professional. Document-ready. IMPORTANT: This is a draft. I will review with Legal and Employee Relations before finalising.

Onboarding & Offboarding

New Hire Onboarding Plan

Per Hire

Use when: A new employee is starting and you need a structured first-week/first-month plan.

Create an onboarding plan for a new [Job Title] joining the [Team] on [Start Date]. Their manager: [name] Buddy/mentor: [name, if assigned] Key tools they'll use: [list: e.g., Jira, Salesforce, Slack, Workday] Structure: - Pre-Day 1: What to send before they start (welcome email, equipment, access requests) - Day 1: Hour-by-hour schedule (orientation, IT setup, team intro, lunch) - Week 1: Day-by-day goals (what they should learn, who they should meet) - 30-Day Milestone: What "success at day 30" looks like - 60-Day Check-in: Questions to ask them about their experience Include a "buddy checklist" — tasks for the assigned buddy to complete with the new hire. Format as a checklist the manager can print and work through.

Exit Interview Summary & Trends

Quarterly

Use when: Analysing exit interview data to find patterns and present to leadership.

Here are exit interview notes from [X] departing employees over [time period]: [Paste anonymised exit interview summaries] Please analyse: 1. Top 3 themes: What are the most common reasons for leaving? 2. Department patterns: Are certain teams over-represented? 3. Sentiment analysis: What's the overall tone? (bitter, neutral, grateful, mixed) 4. Fixable vs. unfixable: Separate reasons we can address from ones we can't (e.g., relocation vs. toxic manager) 5. Quotes: Pull 3-5 anonymised quotes that best capture the themes (for leadership impact) Format as a one-page briefing for the CHRO with: - A headline finding - A data summary table - 3 recommended actions ranked by impact

Policy & Employee Communications

Policy Draft / Update

As Needed

Use when: Creating a new HR policy or updating an existing one.

Draft a [new / updated] policy for: [policy topic: e.g., Remote Work, PTO, Expense Reimbursement, Social Media Usage] Company context: [industry, size, locations, culture: e.g., "mid-size professional services firm, 500 employees, hybrid model"] Key requirements: [List what the policy needs to cover] Must comply with: [relevant regulations: e.g., FMLA, ADA, state-specific laws, GDPR] Include: 1. Purpose: Why this policy exists (1-2 sentences) 2. Scope: Who it applies to 3. Policy statement: The actual rules, written clearly 4. Procedures: Step-by-step process for employees to follow 5. Exceptions: How to request exceptions 6. Effective date and review cadence Write in plain English. If a new employee can't understand a sentence on first read, simplify it. NOTE: This is a first draft. Legal review required before publication.

All-Hands / Company-Wide Email

As Needed

Use when: Communicating a sensitive change (benefits update, layoff, reorg, policy change) to all employees.

Draft a company-wide communication about: [topic] What's happening: [the facts, plainly stated] Why: [the reason behind the change] Who's affected: [everyone / specific teams / specific locations] When: [effective date and timeline] Tone: [empathetic and transparent / upbeat and forward-looking / serious and measured] Must include: - An honest explanation of "why" (employees see through vague corporate-speak) - What changes for them specifically - What does NOT change (people need reassurance) - Where to get answers (FAQ link, manager, HR contact) - A human sign-off from [sender name and title] Must NOT include: [List any sensitive details to exclude] Length: under [300/400] words. Read it like an employee who's anxious about their job — does it answer their real questions?

Onboarding Feedback Summary

Weekly

Use when: Collecting and synthesising onboarding feedback from recent hires.

Here are onboarding survey responses from [X] new hires who started in [month/quarter]: [Paste survey responses] Analyse and give me: 1. Overall satisfaction score (average rating if numerical, or sentiment summary if text) 2. What's working well: Top 3 things new hires consistently praise 3. What needs fixing: Top 3 pain points or complaints, with frequency 4. Quick wins: Issues we could fix this week with minimal effort 5. Systemic issues: Problems that need a process change or investment 6. Verbatim highlights: 3 positive and 3 constructive quotes (anonymised) Format as a one-page summary I can present at our weekly HR standup.
